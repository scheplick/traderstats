The math of risk

Drawdown asymmetry

A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to recover. A 20% loss only needs +25%. Position sizing is everything.

Kelly is brutal

Full Kelly sizing produces a ~50% drawdown in the average path. Most pros use half-Kelly or less.

Edge erodes fast

Average alpha decay for a published equity factor is ~58% post-publication.

Volatility and tape

Big days cluster

Missing the 10 best days in the S&P 500 over 20 years cuts your return roughly in half. Most occur within two weeks of the 10 worst days.

Intraday is noise

Roughly 80% of S&P 500 single-day moves above 1% mean-revert within five trading days.

September drag

September is the only month with a negative average S&P 500 return since 1950 (-0.7%).

Costs that quietly kill returns

Bid-ask drag

Active small-cap traders can pay 1-3% per round-trip in spread plus impact — wiping out most edges.

Tax friction

Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income. Holding more than 1 year can save 10-20 points of after-tax return.

Margin interest

At 8% margin rates, a leveraged long needs to earn ~12% gross just to clear a 4% hurdle.

Behavior beats brilliance

Overconfidence tax

The most active 20% of retail accounts underperform buy-and-hold by ~6% per year (Barber and Odean).

Time in beats timing

$10,000 in the S&P 500 from 2003 to 2023 grew to ~$64k if held; ~$29k if you missed the 10 best days.

Process beats prediction

Top systematic funds have win rates of 50-55%. The edge is in payoff asymmetry and discipline.

Sources: SPIVA, Barber and Odean (2000), McLean and Pontiff (2016), Hartford Funds, S&P Dow Jones Indices. Educational only — not trading advice.
